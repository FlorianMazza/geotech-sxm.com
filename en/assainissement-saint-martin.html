Sewerage & drainage in Saint-Martin: a critical discipline in a tropical environment.

Sewerage and drainage is one of the most sensitive disciplines in civil engineering. In Saint-Martin, its importance is further amplified by the fragility of the natural environments — the lagoon, the coastal wetlands, the beaches — that make up the island's living environment and its tourist appeal. Poor management of wastewater or stormwater can have serious and lasting environmental consequences.

Geotech Caraïbes approaches sewerage and drainage with the rigor the discipline demands: compliance with the applicable regulations, sizing suited to tropical rainfall, selection of materials resistant to the island setting, and rigorous coordination with the local network operators. Our in-depth knowledge of the Saint-Martin territory is a decisive asset in designing and building durable, high-performance sewerage and drainage structures.

Wastewater networks (EU) in Saint-Martin

Wastewater includes domestic water (kitchen, bathroom, toilet) and industrial or commercial water. Its collection, conveyance, and treatment are governed by strict regulations, particularly sensitive in island territories such as Saint-Martin.

Geotech Caraïbes carries out:

  • Laying wastewater pipework in CR8-class PVC or HDPE, from individual connections through to the main collectors
  • Inspection manholes in concrete or polypropylene, allowing inspection and maintenance of the network
  • Pumping stations where the topography does not allow gravity flow — common in the low-lying areas of Saint-Martin
  • Individual connections linking each building to the collective network
  • Leak tests required upon acceptance of any wastewater network, guaranteeing the absence of leaks into the ground

Stormwater networks (EP): a major challenge in Saint-Martin

Stormwater management is a particularly critical challenge in Saint-Martin. The island receives intense tropical rainfall, which can reach several hundred millimeters in a few hours during thunderstorm or hurricane events. This runoff must be collected, stored, and released in a controlled manner to prevent flooding, soil erosion, and pollution of the aquatic environments.

Our stormwater network work includes:

  • Stormwater pipework in various diameters, sized according to the island's reference flows and hydraulic calculation standards
  • Gullies and collection grates positioned at low points and along roadway curbs
  • Concrete or grassed ditches for collecting and conveying runoff across large areas
  • Regulation structures (check valves, weirs) to prevent backflow during heavy rain
  • Outlets and discharge devices compliant with the environmental requirements of the local authorities

Retention basins: managing water at the source

Building retention basins has become a frequent requirement of development permits in Saint-Martin. These structures serve to shave the peak flows during intense rainfall events, temporarily storing a volume of water that is then gradually released back to the natural environment or the public network.

Geotech Caraïbes builds:

  • Open-air basins in fill or in cut, waterproofed with a geomembrane or by treating the base
  • Buried basins in concrete or cellular structures, suited to constrained sites
  • Flow regulation structures (valves, safety weirs) guaranteeing the hydraulic operation of the system
  • Landscaping integrating the basin into its surroundings where it is visible

Off-grid sewerage: solutions for unconnected areas

Not all areas of Saint-Martin are served by a collective sewer network. For isolated buildings or unconnected sectors, off-grid (or non-collective) sewerage is the appropriate regulatory solution.

Geotech Caraïbes carries out the earthworks and installs the off-grid sewerage systems: all-water septic tanks, drainage trenches, and planted filters, in accordance with the requirements of the Public Off-Grid Sewerage Service (SPANC) and the characteristics of the surveyed soil.

Specific constraints of sewerage and drainage in a tropical island setting

Hurricane rainfall: basins and networks sized for 10- or 100-year tropical rainfall must factor in the exceptional flows generated by hurricanes. Irma (2017) was a brutal reminder of the island's vulnerability — our structures are designed to withstand these extreme events.

The sensitivity of the lagoon: the Saint-Martin lagoon is a natural environment of exceptional richness (seagrass beds, mangroves, spawning grounds). Any discharge of insufficiently treated water into this environment is strictly prohibited and heavily penalized. Our sewerage and drainage work systematically factors in this priority environmental constraint.

The rugged topography: Saint-Martin's relief generates steep slopes that promote erosion. Our stormwater networks incorporate energy-dissipation structures (drops, dissipators) to prevent scouring of the outlets.

Low-permeability soils: in many sectors of the island, soil permeability is low, making infiltration difficult. Source-control solutions (swales, drainage trenches) must be carefully studied and sized before implementation.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions
about sewerage & drainage in Saint-Martin.

What sewerage regulations apply in Saint-Martin?

On the French side, Saint-Martin applies the national regulations on collective and non-collective sewerage, adapted to the overseas context. The SPANC (Public Off-Grid Sewerage Service) checks the compliance of off-grid systems. On the Sint Maarten side, specific rules apply under the authority of the local technical services. Geotech Caraïbes has a command of both regulatory frameworks.

Why is a retention basin often required in Saint-Martin?

Because of the intense tropical rainfall and the increasing impermeabilization of soils tied to urbanization, development permits in Saint-Martin frequently require the creation of a retention basin. This structure shaves the peak flows during thunderstorm events, protecting the downstream public networks and the receiving environments (lagoon, sea). The volume is sized according to the impermeabilized area of the project and the reference rainfall.

Can stormwater be infiltrated in Saint-Martin?

Infiltrating stormwater into the ground is possible in certain sectors of Saint-Martin, notably on permeable land away from sensitive coastal zones. It is subject to a preliminary soil study (permeability test) and the requirements of the local authorities. In many cases, the low permeability of the soil or the sensitivity of the subsoil rules out this solution, which must then be replaced by a regulated discharge to the network or the natural environment.

How is sewerage handled for a villa in an unconnected area of Saint-Martin?

For a villa in an area not connected to the collective network, off-grid sewerage is the regulatory solution. Geotech Caraïbes carries out the soil study, proposes a suitable system (all-water septic tank with drainage trenches, or a compact filter), and handles the installation in compliance with the SPANC requirements. A compliant installation is essential to obtain the certificate of compliance and for the later resale of the property.

What materials do you use for sewer pipework?

We mainly use CR8-class PVC pipework (for standard wastewater and stormwater networks) and HDPE (high-density polyethylene, for pressure lines and aggressive environments). These materials are corrosion-resistant — an essential asset in the tropical, marine environment of Saint-Martin. The manholes and ancillary structures are in precast concrete or polypropylene depending on site conditions.

Do you also carry out sewerage and drainage work in Sint Maarten?

Yes, Geotech Caraïbes works across the entire island, the French side as well as the Dutch side. The sewerage and drainage challenges are shared by both parts of the island — protecting the shared lagoon, managing stormwater during hurricanes — and our expertise applies to both contexts with the same technical rigor.
