Sewerage & drainage

Stormwater management: why EP networks are essential in Saint-Martin

Published on May 10, 2025 · Geotech Caraïbes

With rainfall intensities that can exceed 100 mm/h during tropical events, Saint-Martin is especially exposed to runoff and flooding. Stormwater (EP) management is not a decorative option: it is a technical and regulatory necessity.

Why this matters so much on the island

The pronounced relief quickly funnels water toward the low points, which are often built up. A poorly drained parcel passes the problem on to neighboring plots and weakens structures. Controlling rainwater protects both your building and the project owner's liability.

Sizing an EP network

Sizing rests on three elements: the design rainfall intensity (return period), the project's active surface area, and the runoff coefficient (a roof or an asphalt surface sheds far more water than a green space). From this data, the flows to be evacuated and the section of the pipework or retention structures are calculated.

The most common solutions

  • Swales and ditches: open-air collection and slowing of water;
  • Buried EP networks: sized pipework, manholes and outfalls;
  • Retention basins: temporary storage to shave off peak flows;
  • Infiltration: where the soil allows, returning water to the natural environment.

Plan ahead during earthworks

Slopes, subgrade and trenches are prepared during the earthworks phase. Integrating the EP network from the outset avoids costly rework and ensures efficient gravity flow.
