Demolition, deconstruction, soft strip: what are we talking about?

Demolition covers all the operations aimed at removing all or part of a built structure in order to free up the land or prepare a renovation. Depending on the nature of the project, it goes hand in hand with a soft strip, material sorting, and finishing earthworks. These complementary services follow a precise logic:

  • Full demolition: deconstruction of the building, slabs, foundations, and structures in concrete, masonry, or framing, right through to the complete clearing of the plot.
  • Soft strip before demolition: selective removal of interior elements (partitions, finishes, joinery, fixtures) to isolate the materials and prepare a clean demolition.
  • Selective waste sorting and haul-off: separating rubble, metals, wood, and other materials, then hauling them off to approved channels, in compliance with local environmental regulations.
  • Platform earthworks: once the site is cleared, backfilling, grading to level, and compaction to deliver a stable platform, ready to receive the new project.

Our demolition services in Saint-Martin and Sint Maarten

Geotech Caraïbes covers the entire demolition chain, from the initial study to the handover of a clean, build-ready plot:

  • Full demolition — buildings, slabs, concrete and masonry structures, ancillary works. We adapt the methods (mechanical demolition, hydraulic breaker) to the nature of the structure and its immediate surroundings.

  • Jobsite securing — marking out, protection of neighboring properties, access management, and risk prevention throughout the operation.
  • Work on constrained sites — difficult access, narrow plots, party walls, dense urban settings: our crews and our equipment (mini-excavators, compact machines) are suited to the island's complex work areas.

The specific constraints of demolition in Saint-Martin

The post-hurricane context: many of the island's buildings have been weakened by hurricane events. Demolishing a damaged structure requires a precise reading of its residual stability and a rigorous order of operations to avoid any uncontrolled collapse.

Party walls and constrained sites: in urban areas or dense subdivisions, the immediate proximity of other buildings calls for gentle, gradual demolition methods, strict marking out, and systematic protection of neighboring properties.

Tropical constraints: hurricane season from June to November, heavy rain, heat, and dust: every demolition jobsite incorporates dust suppression, water management, and the securing of open work areas, in anticipation of the climate hazards specific to the Caribbean arc.

Waste management in an island setting: hauling off rubble and materials is a major challenge on an island. Geotech Caraïbes relies on a network of approved local channels to sort, recover, and haul off waste in strict compliance with the regulations in force in Saint-Martin and Sint Maarten.

Our process: from diagnosis to delivered platform

Every demolition jobsite follows a structured process that guarantees safety, a clean site, and compliance with the specifications:

  1. Diagnosis and site study: field visit, analysis of the structure and its stability, locating of networks, party walls, and access constraints. Definition of the method and a detailed quote.
  2. Securing and soft strip: marking out, protection of neighboring properties, disconnection of utilities, then selective soft strip of the finishing trades before attacking the structure.
  3. Controlled demolition: gradual deconstruction of the building and structures, in a studied order, with equipment suited to the work area and the jobsite environment.
  4. Sorting and haul-off: separating the materials, loading and hauling off the rubble to approved channels, and cleaning the work area.
  5. Earthworks and platform: backfilling, grading to level, and compaction to deliver a sound, stable plot ready to receive the new project.

The cost of a demolition depends on many factors: the nature and floor area of the structure, the materials (concrete, masonry, framing), site accessibility, party walls, the volume of waste to be hauled off, and any platform earthworks after demolition. How is demolition waste managed and hauled off?

Waste is sorted directly on the jobsite: inert rubble, scrap metal, wood, and other streams are separated and then transported to approved channels in Saint-Martin and Sint Maarten.
